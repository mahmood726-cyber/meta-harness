TITLE: Impact of premastectomy versus postmastectomy radiotherapy on postoperative complications in breast reconstruction: A systematic review and meta-analysis. 
PUBLICATION TYPES: ["research-article", "Journal Article"]
ARTICLE TYPE: research-article
ABSTRACT:
Background The present study aimed to performed a systemic review and meta-analysis to compare. Impact of premastectomy radiotherapy (Pre-MRT) versus Postmastectomy radiotherapy (PMRT) on Postoperative Complications in Breast Reconstruction. Methods PubMed, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library were searched from inception to February 3, 2026. Eligible studies compared Pre-MRT and PMRT for postoperative complications following breast reconstruction. Random-effects models were used to estimate pooled risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Results Three studies, including one randomized-controlled trial and two cohort studies comprising a total of 483 breasts were included in the meta-analysis. The meta-analysis demonstrated no significant difference in the rate of postoperative complications between the PreMRT and PMRT groups (pooled RR: 1.36; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.90–2.06; p = 0.0583). Subgroup analyses demonstrated no statistically significant differences observed in the rates of complete flap loss, skin flap necrosis, local recurrence, hematoma, seroma, infection, implant loss, or complete nipple necrosis. Conclusion This meta-analysis did not demonstrate a statistically significant difference in postoperative complication rates between PreMRT and PMRT in patients undergoing breast reconstruction. However, given the limited number of included studies and the imprecision of the pooled estimates, these findings should be interpreted with caution.
METHODS:
Methods This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions (version 6.5). and reported following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. 12 The study protocol was prospectively registered in the PROSPERO (registration number: CRD420261360579). Search strategy Two investigators (AK and PEK) systematically searched PubMed, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library from database inception to February 3, 2026. The detailed search strategies applied to each database are described below: (‘Breast cancer’ OR ‘Breast carcinoma’) AND (‘premastectomy’ OR ‘pre-mastectomy’) AND (‘postmastectomy’ OR ‘post-mastectomy’). No restrictions were placed on study design, publication date, or language. Gray literature, including conference abstracts, was also considered. Additionally, a snowballing approach was employed through manual screening of reference lists and citation tracking of included studies and relevant reviews to identify any additional eligible publications. Study selection and eligibility criteria This meta-analysis was designed based on the PICO framework (Population, Intervention, Comparison, and Outcome). The components were defined as follows: P: patients with breast cancer undergoing mastectomy, radiotherapy and reconstruction surgery; I: premastectomy radiotherapy; C: postmastectomy radiotherapy; O: postoperative complication rates. The inclusion criteria for this meta-analysis were as follows: (1) studies involving patients with breast cancer who underwent mastectomy, radiotherapy and reconstruction surgery; (2) studies comparing the complications of PreMRT and PMRT (3) a control group consisting of patients receiving PMRT (4) studies reporting data on postoperative complications; and cosmetic and functional Assessment (5) eligible study designs including cohort studies and randomized or non-RCT. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) conference abstracts; (2) review articles, editorials, and letters to the editor; (3) studies without available full texts; (4) studies that did not compare PreMRT and PMRT; (5) studies that did not report complication outcomes; and (6) duplicate publications or studies with overlapping patient populations. Two investigators (AK and PEK) independently screened the titles and abstracts of retrieved records to assess eligibility. Full texts of potentially relevant articles were subsequently reviewed according to the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Any disagreements were resolved through discussion until consensus was reached. Data extraction and quality assessment Data were independently extracted by two investigators (AK and PEK) from the selected studies using a standardized form. The extracted information included study design, the name of the first author, publication year, country, and sample sizes for both study and control groups. Furthermore, we recorded specific details regarding the type of surgery, main findings of each study. Statistical outcomes, such as risk estimates (incidence rates) along with their 95% confidence intervals (CIs), and postoperative complications were also documented. The methodological quality of the included studies was assessed using standardized tools according to their design. For the cohort studies, the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) was employed, 13 encompassing three key domains: quality of selection, comparability between groups, and outcomes of study participants. Studies scoring 7 to 9 were considered to have a low risk of bias, scores of 4 to 6 a moderate risk, and scores of <4 a high risk. For the RCT included in this review, the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (RoB 2.0) was applied to evaluate its internal validity across five domains (randomization process, adherence to the intervention, missing outcome data, outcome measurement, and selective reporting), and then provided an overall risk of bias judgment). Any discrepancies during the extraction or assessment process were resolved through consensus or discussion. Primary outcome and secondary outcomes The primary outcome was postoperative complications, including complete flap loss, skin flap necrosis, local recurrence, hematoma, seroma, infection events, implant loss and complete nipple necrosis. The secondary outcome was patient-reported outcomes after radiotherapy, evaluated using the Satisfaction with Cosmetic Outcome Questionnaire. Data analyses and software All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.5.1) within the RStudio environment. For dichotomous outcomes, pooled risk ratios (RRs) with corresponding 95% CIs were calculated to compare complication rates between patients receiving PreMRT and those receiving PMRT. The evaluated complications included complete flap loss, skin flap necrosis, local recurrence, hematoma, seroma, infection, implant loss, and complete nipple necrosis. Meta-analyses were performed using the inverse-variance method under a random-effects model. Given the small number of included studies, fixed-effect models were additionally performed as sensitivity analyses to assess the robustness of the pooled estimates. A leave-one-out sensitivity analysis was also conducted for the overall postoperative complication outcome by sequentially excluding each study and recalculating the pooled effect estimate to assess the influence of individual studies on the overall result. Between-study heterogeneity was quantified using the I² statistic. 14 An I² value of less than 40% was interpreted as low heterogeneity, values between 40% and 75% as moderate heterogeneity, and values greater than 75% as substantial heterogeneity. Publication bias was evaluated using funnel plot inspection when at least ten studies were available for each outcome. 15 Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided p value < 0.05. For rare-event outcomes, studies with zero events in one treatment arm were handled using a continuity correction of 0.5 during the calculation of risk ratios. Studies with zero events in both treatment arms were excluded from the pooled risk ratio analyses because they do not contribute to the estimation of relative treatment effects.
