TITLE: Preemptive mechanical circulatory support in ventricular tachycardia ablation: A systematic review and meta-analysis.
PUBLICATION TYPES: ["research-article", "Journal Article"]
ARTICLE TYPE: research-article
ABSTRACT:
Background The benefit of preemptive mechanical circulatory support (MCS) for ventricular tachycardia (VT) ablation remains unclear. This meta-analysis compared the efficacy and safety of preemptive MCS to standard care for VT ablation. Methods An unrestricted literature search of PubMed, Embase, and Web of Science identified studies on preemptive MCS for VT ablation. MCS included percutaneous left ventricular assist devices or extracorporeal membrane oxygenation. The primary endpoint was all-cause mortality; secondary endpoints included VT recurrence, procedural time, procedural complications (vascular access, bleeding, stroke, pericardial effusion, heart failure, myocardial infarction), and 30-day major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE). Results Eight studies with 2,262 patients (414 with MCS, 1,848 without) were included. Mean follow-up was 15.8 months, mean age was 62.6 years, 12.5% were women, and mean PAINESD scores were 15.7 in the MCS group and 10.5 in the no-MCS group. There was no significant difference in all-cause mortality (OR 1.58, 95% CI 0.60–4.19, p = 0.36, I²=81%) or VT recurrence (OR 1.03, 95% CI 0.68–1.56, p = 0.90, I²=46%). Preemptive MCS was associated with longer procedural times (mean difference 46.0 min, 95% CI 11.1–80.8, p = 0.01, I²=86%), more procedural complications—driven by vascular access and pericardial effusion (OR 2.25, 95% CI 1.26–4.03, p = 0.006, I²=33%). Thirty-day MACE was not uniformly defined across studies and was therefore not pooled. Conclusions Preemptive MCS was not associated with a significant difference in all-cause mortality or VT recurrence in patients undergoing VT ablation but was associated with longer procedures, and more procedural complications. The certainty of evidence was very low, reflecting the observational study design, baseline imbalance between groups, and heterogeneity; these findings do not preclude the selective use of MCS in high-risk patients and require confirmation in randomized trials. METHODS:
Methods Data search The systematic review was conducted in May 2026 using a preplanned protocol in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) statement. A review protocol was not prospectively registered. Databases searched included PubMed, Web of Science, and Embase. Search terms included combinations of the following keywords and MeSH terms: (“Ablation”) AND (“mechanical circulatory support” OR “ecmo” OR “impella”) NOT (Review[publication type]). The search was not restricted by time or publication status. In addition, reference lists of selected articles were screened for relevant citations that may have been missed by electronic searches. All study designs were eligible for inclusion, including retrospective studies, prospective cohorts, registries, and randomized controlled trials. The protocol was developed prior to data extraction but was not prospectively registered. Study selection Studies were screened independently by two investigators (MN, SM) at the level of title and abstract, followed by full-text review for eligibility. Disagreements were resolved by consensus with involvement of a third investigator (DTZ). Inclusion criteria were studies evaluating the use of preemptive MCS during ventricular tachycardia ablation and reporting clinical outcomes comparing patients who received MCS with those who underwent ablation without MCS. MCS included percutaneous left ventricular assist devices (pLVAD) or extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) used prophylactically prior to or at the start of the ablation procedure. Intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP) was excluded as a source of MCS, as IABP provides relatively limited hemodynamic support compared with other percutaneous MCS devices [ 7 ]. Exclusion criteria included review articles, case reports, conference abstracts without sufficient outcome data, studies not reporting comparative outcomes, or studies evaluating only rescue MCS after hemodynamic collapse. In all included cohorts, mechanical circulatory support was in place before or at the start of the ablation procedure, including studies in which support had been established for cardiogenic shock or refractory electrical storm prior to ablation. Studies not published in English were also excluded. When multiple studies reported overlapping patient cohorts from the same center, we retained the study providing the most complete data for the prespecified outcomes to avoid duplicate inclusion of patients. Data extraction Data extraction was performed independently by two investigators, and discrepancies were resolved by consensus. A standardized data collection form was used to record study characteristics including publication year, study design, number of participants, patient demographics, type of MCS, and follow-up duration. Clinical outcomes extracted included all-cause mortality, VT recurrence, procedural time, procedural complications, and 30-day major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE). All-cause mortality was captured at the follow-up interval reported by each study, which varied across studies and did not correspond to a uniform time point (Supplementary Table 1 ). Procedural complications included vascular access complications, bleeding, stroke, pericardial effusion, heart failure, and myocardial infarction. For the pooled analysis, procedural complications were counted as the number of patients with at least one complication in each arm rather than the total number of complication events. Study quality was assessed independently by two investigators using the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale for cohort studies, which evaluates the selection, comparability, and outcome domains for a maximum of nine stars, with discrepancies resolved by consensus. The certainty of evidence for each outcome was appraised using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) framework, with observational evidence beginning at low certainty and rated for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias. Statistical analysis Meta-analysis of summary statistics from individual studies was performed using Cochrane Review Manager Version 5.4. Dichotomous outcomes were expressed as odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI), while continuous outcomes were expressed as mean differences (MD) with 95% CI. A random-effects model was used to account for potential heterogeneity among included studies. Heterogeneity was assessed using the I² statistic, with values of 0–40% considered low heterogeneity, 40–60% moderate heterogeneity, 60–90% substantial heterogeneity, and > 90% considerable heterogeneity. Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided p-value < 0.05. To explore sources of heterogeneity, we performed a subgroup analysis restricted to studies using percutaneous left ventricular assist devices; studies using venoarterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation or mixed device strategies were too few to pool separately. We further performed leave-one-out sensitivity analyses, sequentially omitting each study, to evaluate the influence of individual studies on the pooled estimates and on between-study heterogeneity.
