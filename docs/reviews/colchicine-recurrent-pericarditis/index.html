  • Evidence base incomplete. The registered PICO is broad (acute first episode OR recurrent pericarditis, treated to prevent recurrence), so ICAP — a first-attack acute-pericarditis trial — is ELIGIBLE and is NOT refused for population. It is not pooled only because its recurrence-specific outcome is not extractable from an accessible source: its abstract reports only the incessant-or-recurrent composite (a different endpoint) and its NEJM full text is not open-access. This pool is therefore the two recurrent-pericarditis trials (CORP and CORP-2), which agree with no between-study heterogeneity and both favour colchicine; the conventional common-effect interval shown alongside the HKSJ interval excludes no-effect, and a later randomised-evidence meta-analysis that includes ICAP reports essentially the same point estimate. The wide HKSJ interval is a two-trial small-sample artefact, not evidence that colchicine is ineffective — adding ICAP (were its recurrence component retrievable) would tighten the interval to exclude no-effect.
