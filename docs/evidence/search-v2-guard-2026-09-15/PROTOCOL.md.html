# Protocol: search_v2 discovery-query guard, sealed-vocabulary exemption (2026-09-15)

**Status: RETROSPECTIVE.** This protocol is authored AFTER the outcome it governs was observed. The
sealed 21-topic measurement (`docs/evidence/search-v2-measurement-2026-09-15`, engine blob
`3652170341b73593bab949f815d15e2d58f9f4ec`) returned RAN_ERROR on 5 of 21 topics because the
engine's own anti-name-seeding guard refused each topic's query. The refusing tokens were known
before one line of this protocol was written. Nothing below is pre-specification; it is a rule
stated in full, with both answers it yields disclosed, before the re-run that the rule governs.

hyphen or a digit, or is all-caps and >= 4 characters, unless it is in the hand-maintained set

through (`BAY`, `BIBR1048`, `DU-176B`, ...) and, correctly, did not grow it for the sealed

not a rule. Topic 33 fails again.

2026-09-14, before the development/measurement split was sealed (41a466fb, 2026-09-15 02:30 +0100)

configs are byte-identical to their blobs at 41a466fb (`git rev-parse 41a466fb:topics/<slug>.json`
== `git hash-object topics/<slug>.json`, 32 of 32).

## The rule

A discovery query emitted by search_v2 is allowed iff, for every token the acronym heuristic flags:

1. **Sealed-vocabulary exemption.** The token is covered by a term in the topic's registered
   vocabulary (`intervention_agents` keys and synonyms, `intervention_class_terms`,
   `intervention_terms` fallback, `include.population_any`, `comparator_terms`), where covered
   means the folded token equals the folded term or is one of its whitespace-delimited words;
   AND the topic's vocabulary is sealed: `registry/search_vocabulary_seal.json` carries, for the
   slug, the sha256 of the canonical JSON of exactly those fields, and the working-tree config
   hashes to the same value. A vocabulary that has drifted from its seal, or a topic with no seal
   entry, gets no exemption and the guard behaves exactly as before (refuse).
2. **Benchmark-acronym collision, checked at SEAL time, never by the engine.** No sealed
   vocabulary term equals (lower-cased, whole term) the registered acronym of any benchmark
   positive. `scripts/seal_search_vocabulary.py` builds the collision set from
   `registry/search_benchmark.json` positives whose acronym is resolvable: the CT.gov `acronym`
   field of a cached record sharing the positive's NCT or PMID, or a `trial` name that is itself
   acronym-shaped (<= 20 chars, all caps, no digits, no spaces); it writes any collision into the
   slug's seal row, and `harness/search_v2.sealed_vocabulary` grants a slug with a non-empty
   collision list NO exemption. The engine itself never opens the benchmark: an earlier draft of
   this rule read it from inside `build_queries` and `tests/test_search_benchmark_isolation.py`
   did not fire because the path was built with `os.path.join`; the detector now carries the
   bare basenames and a plant for that form. A positive whose acronym cannot be resolved is NOT
   covered by this check; the count covered is written into the seal and echoed by every run.
3. **Everything else is unchanged.** Tokens not covered by sealed vocabulary are refused as
   NAME_SEEDED; identifier, DOI, PMID, `[uid]`, `[Title]` and journal+year features are refused
   as before; the public `classify_query(query)` with no vocabulary context is byte-for-byte the
   classifier it was, so no served page moves because of this rule.

The seal is the registration: `registry/search_vocabulary_seal.json` is computed from the configs
at 41a466fb (the split-seal commit), not from the working tree, and a unit test refuses if any
sealed slug's working-tree vocabulary hashes differently. Re-sealing is an explicit, named event.

## Both answers, disclosed

Under the guard as it stood (rule = hand-maintained allowlist): the five topics are RAN_ERROR,
21-topic capability reads "audit-found 1 of 12; pooled-or-declared 120 of 135; 5 of 21 RAN_ERROR".
That number is in `docs/evidence/search-v2-measurement-2026-09-15` and stays there.

Under the rule above: the five queries are allowed (each offending token is covered by sealed
vocabulary and collides with no resolvable benchmark acronym — MEASURED on the sealed configs
before the code was written; the collision set at authoring time had 11 resolvable acronyms:
CLEAR SYNERGY, EMPA-VISION, FISSH, FLOW, FREEDOM-CVO, ICAP, IRON-HF, J-EMPHASIS-HF, PHILO,
SONIA, SOUL, none of which is a vocabulary term of any of the 32 topics). What the five topics
then RETRIEVE is not known at authoring time and is reported, whatever it is, in
`docs/evidence/search-v2-measurement-2026-09-15` as a second run against the same sealed
benchmark, with the first run's numbers left in place beside it.

## What this rule does not do

- It does not make the guard stronger against a trial name smuggled into a topic config AFTER
  sealing: that path is closed by the seal test, not by lexical judgement.
- It does not make the guard stronger against a trial name that was in a config BEFORE sealing
  and whose acronym is not resolvable from cached records (267 of 278 positives at authoring
  time). For those the defence is that the configs predate the engine, which is a provenance
  claim (MEASURED above), not a detector.
- It does not touch the DEVELOPMENT allowlist entries lane S3 added; they remain in
  `_NON_TRIAL_ACRONYMS` and are now redundant for sealed topics. They are not removed in this
  change because removing them would re-render the crystalloids page (`Plasma-Lyte`) a second
  time; a later change may retire them behind its own before/after.

## Plants (must fire before the fix and after it)

- P2: a config whose population term is `FISSH` (a benchmark positive's acronym) -> refused

- P4: the five real topics' queries -> refused before (the known answer), allowed after
  (the intended change). P4 is the change; P1-P3 are the proof the detector is still armed.
