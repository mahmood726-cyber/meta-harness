Meta-harness: reproducibility and evidence audit, with stated limits

A research-stage, open-source workflow for inspecting and rerunning meta-analysis inputs, decisions and outputs. This is a research and teaching invitation, not a validation certificate.

Project repository · Technical overview · Claims and limitations

Critical scope distinction. Internal replay from committed analysis inputs is not the same as reproduction from a protocol-only registration commit. The legacy review corpus does not establish registration-time reproduction, independently complete literature searching, accurate eligibility, or prospectively validated autonomous review performance. It is an adversarial regression corpus.

Three resources for evidence-synthesis authors

Meta-harness

Links committed source material, screening and extraction records, documented analytical choices and review pages. The aim is to make methods and source-to-result paths more inspectable. Read the source and limitations.

AllMeta

A collection of browser-based tools for study-level evidence synthesis, including pairwise and network meta-analysis workspaces. Suitability and numerical correctness must be checked for each method and use case. Explore AllMeta.

Synthesis Courses

Free interactive educational material covering systematic reviews, pairwise and network meta-analysis, individual participant data, and related methods. Explore Synthesis Courses.

A proposed collaboration

For one outcome from a published systematic review or meta-analysis, we can propose a transparent reproducibility exercise using author-shared or publicly available extraction data. A completed exercise would identify the data sources, eligibility decisions, estimand, model, software versions, and differences from published results. Any plot or estimate would be described as a reproduction only after a method-matched check; otherwise as an exploratory reanalysis.

What the current evidence does—and does not—support

  • Internal computational replay: the repository reports conditional deterministic recomputability from committed analysis inputs on the legacy corpus. This does not establish evidence-set correctness or completeness.
  • Registration-time reproduction: not established for the legacy reviews, which lacked protocol-only registration commits pinning all later inputs.
  • Search completeness: most legacy retrieval was known-item or title-seeded rather than autonomous discovery; the project does not claim systematic independent search recovery.
  • Model-family comparisons: agreement across AI systems is a consistency observation, not independent human adjudication or proof of externally verified superiority.
  • Source-location checks: locating displayed digits in a committed source does not prove the correct endpoint, population, analysis set, or design was used.
  • Published comparators: inability to extract machine-readable numbers from one source does not prove that its figures, supplements, datasets, or code cannot be verified.

Clinical and publication safeguard: these resources do not replace independently validated analysis software, a complete systematic search, critical appraisal, specialist statistical review, or clinical judgement.
