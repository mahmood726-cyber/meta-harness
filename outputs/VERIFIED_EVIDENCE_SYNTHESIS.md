## Three cautions — recorded so nobody implements them naively
- **The uncertainty budget risks being pseudo-quantitative.** "Search completeness uncertainty: MODERATE" is a judgement dressed as a measurement. The **robustness envelope** — N defensible specifications, the range of HR across them, how many favour treatment, how many exclude 1 — is real and buildable (the glp1 strands, the k=8/9/10/11 panel, RR-sensitivity, leave-one-out are its rows). **Build the envelope. Do not ship the ordinal budget unless every row has a defined, measured basis.**
- **Capture–recapture for search completeness is dangerous — do not build it.** It assumes independence between retrieval channels; PubMed, CENTRAL, registries and published metas are heavily correlated. It would produce a number people quote as completeness. **Report channel overlap descriptively and test recall with hidden sentinel trials** (the retrieve-then-reject controls and the REACT benchmark sets are exactly that) — honest and falsifiable. Never estimate an unseen population.
- **The evidence/decision separation is the most important item and is buried at the end.** Publish the effect, absolute reduction, certainty, harms and represented populations; keep baseline risk, burden, cost and preferences in a separate decision layer. That is the ethical line; elevate it to the front of any implementation.

Almost every proposal consumes **the trial-family node** and **the claim graph**. Those two are the foundation; the rest are consumers. Anything built before them is rebuilt.
